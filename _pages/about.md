Hi! I'm **Noah Lee**, a machine learning researcher at [MIT Lincoln Laboratory](https://www.ll.mit.edu/) in Lexington, MA. I work at the intersection of computer vision, foundation models, and multi-sensor imaging.

My research focuses on applying large multimodal foundation models to challenging real-world sensing problems:

- **Vision-language models (VLMs) for few-shot detection** — benchmarking foundation models on satellite hyperspectral imagery under limited data, extending them to open-set object detection in X-ray imagery, and analyzing scaling laws with few-shot samples.
- **Foundation models for egocentric action recognition** — long-term action recognition on body camera footage, with structured generation for pseudo-labeling unlabeled video.
- **3D object segmentation in CT volumes** — pipelines that detect hazardous materials in luggage at security checkpoints, lifting 2D detection into 3D voxel segmentations.
- **Interactive imaging for passenger checkpoint screening** — microservice pipelines, finetuned radar anomaly detection, and self-supervised pose models from millimeter-wave radar.

I'm also an **MSCE student at Texas A&M University** (Fall 2026 – Spring 2028), where I'm working in the [Safety Systems AI Lab](https://ssai-lab.github.io) under Dr. Namgyun Kim on using vision-language models and computer vision to interpret and vectorize construction blueprint PDFs.

Previously, I collaborated with the **DAF-MIT AI Accelerator** at MIT on pilot performance prediction from multimodal physiological signals, and I've worked as a Systems Engineer co-op at **The MITRE Corporation**.

I received my **B.S. in Computer Science** from Northeastern University, graduating *Summa Cum Laude* with a 3.96 GPA.
